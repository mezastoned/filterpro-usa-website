Combustible Dust Testing & Mitigation

Many process dusts — wood, metal, food, plastics, composites — can explode when suspended in air near an ignition source. NFPA standards require facilities that handle combustible dusts to analyze that hazard, and OSHA enforces combustible dust safety through its national emphasis program. The starting point is knowing your material's kST value — the deflagration index that measures how violently your specific dust explodes. We arrange testing to determine it, then walk you through practical mitigation strategies so your facility moves toward compliance with a clear, prioritized plan instead of guesswork.

  • kST value dust testing
  • Explosion hazard evaluation
  • OSHA & NFPA standards guidance

Common Questions

Straight answers about industrial filtration

What is a kST value, and why does it matter?
kST is the deflagration index — a lab-measured number that describes how violently your specific dust explodes when suspended in air and ignited. It's the foundation of combustible dust safety: explosion vents, suppression systems, and isolation devices are all sized based on it. If you generate wood, metal, food, plastic, or composite dust and don't know your kST value, that's the first thing to fix.
Is combustible dust testing actually required?
NFPA standards require facilities handling combustible dusts to conduct a dust hazard analysis, and OSHA enforces combustible dust safety through its national emphasis program and the general duty clause — citations routinely follow incidents and inspections. Beyond compliance, the risk is real: combustible dust explosions injure workers and destroy facilities every year. Testing is the inexpensive first step that tells you how seriously your dust needs to be managed.
